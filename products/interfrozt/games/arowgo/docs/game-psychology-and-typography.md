# Enjoyable thinking, essential typography

Reviewed 10 November 2026. This is a design rationale, not evidence that AROWGO changes dopamine, intelligence or mental health.

## Research and successful-game references

- [Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan: A Motivational Model of Video Game Engagement (2010)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2010_PrzybylskiRigbyRyan_ROGP.pdf) relates enjoyment to competence, autonomy and relatedness. Application: legible goals, understandable feedback, optional assistance and player choice. This is an inference for AROWGO, not a test result for our game.
- [ustwo: Monument Valley](https://ustwo.com/work/monument-valley/) describes novelty, delight, accessible mechanics and a complete experience without grinding or real failure. The studio documents design awards. Application: let the beautiful puzzle lead; keep instruction secondary; provide a calm stopping point. We do not infer its success was caused by one typography choice.
- [King: Candy Crush Saga — Collect the orders](https://candycrush.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004468849-Game-Modes-Collect-the-orders) describes an objective introduced before a level and remaining orders visible during play. This is a useful comparison for concrete goals and visible progress. Application: recognizable tools, visible progress and a brief completion payoff. Monetization, lives gating and reward pressure are not being copied.
- [National Institute on Aging: Cognitive health and older adults](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/brain-health/cognitive-health-and-older-adults) cautions that commercial brain-training apps do not have sufficient evidence for the same cognitive impact as specific researched training. Findings from such interventions cannot be assigned to AROWGO.

## Motto

**A little play. A little practice in thinking.**

The requested idea, “This game improves your thinking psychology,” is an aspiration, not a substantiated outcome. Current copy describes an activity: examining directions, planning an order and trying alternatives. Do not claim higher IQ, better mental health or general cognitive improvement without appropriate evidence.

## Implemented hierarchy

- Satoshi remains the only UI family, with system sans-serif fallback.
- One centered board. Removed decorative side essays and redundant footer branding.
- Level title: 40–48 px, upright heavy weight. World: 16–17 px.
- Tools: 17–18 px. Primary result action: 16–17 px. Context feedback: 14–15 px.
- Essential result support: 14–16 px. No tiny all-caps captions under familiar hearts/coins.
- Removed tool descriptions, repeated reward text, victory eyebrow, repeated completed-path summary and persistent saved-state caption. Actual save errors remain visible.
- Tool assistance policy remains in Settings. Numeric star/reward summaries remain screen-reader accessible while the visual result uses stars and the animated coin count.
- Shortened encouragement to one sentence. The welcome space now carries the single motto.

## Design priorities and next evidence

Keep clear goals, meaningful choices, a learnable difficulty curve, forgiving retry and a satisfying finish. More effects alone do not establish enjoyment. This build still has one authored level; long-term replay enjoyment requires additional well-paced content and user testing.

Next: observe new players completing a first session; ask about clarity, enjoyment and frustration; measure unnecessary mis-taps, help comprehension and willingness to return voluntarily. Do not treat maximum session duration as the sole success measure. No claim that any game is pleasant for every player at all times is made.
